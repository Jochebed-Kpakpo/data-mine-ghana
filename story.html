GHANA’S ENERGY STORY · 2000–2024

A growing system in the middle of a transition.

Ghana is using more energy, but the sources supplying it have changed significantly. This is the story behind the numbers.

Reading guide

Each chapter focuses on one change and the evidence supporting it.

Explore every year →
01

Biomass defined the system.

In 2000, Ghana’s total energy supply was 6,666 ktoe. Biomass contributed 4,410 ktoe, approximately two-thirds of the entire system. Oil supplied about one-quarter, while natural gas and solar were absent from the reported mix.

66.2%Biomass share of total energy supply in 2000
02

Natural gas changed the structure.

Natural gas entered the national series at scale during the 2010s. Its contribution increased from 5 ktoe in 2009 to 3,906 ktoe in 2024. This was the largest structural addition to Ghana’s energy mix across the period.

28.4%Natural gas share of total supply in 2024
03

Growth produced a more balanced mix.

By 2024, total supply reached 13,741 ktoe. Oil, natural gas and biomass each held substantial shares. Biomass remained important, but its relative position fell as the national system expanded.

+106%Growth in total energy supply between 2000 and 2024
04

Solar is growing from a very small base.

Solar first appears in this supply series in 2016 at 2 ktoe and reaches 16 ktoe in 2024. That is rapid proportional growth, yet solar still represents only 0.11% of reported national energy supply.

0.11%Solar share of total energy supply in 2024
05

The next question is how future demand will be met.

The historical record establishes that demand is rising and the mix can change. It does not, by itself, determine which technologies are bankable or which policies will succeed. Those decisions require capacity, price, project, grid, reliability and investment evidence.
